## 1. This Week's Market Action (Week ending Sept 4)

**Mainland A-shares — lower, with a sector rotation.** China stocks rose on Friday (CSI300 and Shanghai Composite both +0.4% by midday) but were on track to **end the week lower**, as the AI-share rally lost momentum and higher US yields weighed. For the week, the **CSI300 closed nearly 1% down** and the **STAR50 fell ~4%**. The tell-tale sign was **rotation**: consumer staples led onshore gains (+2.6%) as sentiment toward AI supply-chain stocks cooled, with **Kweichow Moutai +2.4%**. The CSI Artificial Intelligence Index edged down 0.2% (FMT/Reuters, Sept 4).

**Hong Kong — resilient, rebounding.** The **Hang Seng rose ~0.6% for the week**, snapping back on Friday (+2.1%) as **HK-listed tech majors rebounded ~2.5% from a two-month low**. **Alibaba +3.4%** and **Xiaomi +3.1%** (on a deal with German auto dealers). This was a recovery from the **prior week (ending Aug 28), when the HSI posted a weekly loss on an Alibaba share-placement shock** (FMT, Sept 4; 巴士的報).

> **Flagged nuance:** Mainland and HK diverged this week — A-shares fell (AI de-rating) while HK rose (tech rebound + weaker USD). Not a contradiction; different index compositions and flow dynamics.

## 2. Macro Backdrop: Solid PMI, Slowing GDP

The August data released this week (Sept 1) was **mixed but tilted positive on activity, negative on the growth trend** (Reuters, Sept 1):

- **RatingDog China General Manufacturing PMI rose to 51.5 in August** from 50.9 in July, beating the 51.0 Reuters consensus — the **longest expansion streak in five years**.
- **Output rose at the fastest pace in three months**; **new export business posted its sharpest rise in six months**; work backlogs hit their fastest accumulation since March.
- **But:** manufacturers **cut output prices for the first time this year** (intense competition, discounting); **employment was unchanged**; and **12-month confidence slipped to its softest since January**.
- **The growth trend is the concern: Q2 GDP slowed to 4.3%**, the weakest in more than three years and below forecasts (Q1 was 5.0%). Weak domestic demand and external uncertainties (trade tensions, geopolitics) continue to cloud the outlook.

**Read-through:** the economy is "muddling through" — private-sector activity is holding up (helped by exports), but the underlying growth trajectory is decelerating and price competition is intensifying.

## 3. External Factors: US Yields, the Fed, and Tariffs

- **Higher US Treasury yields** were the key external drag this week, weighing on global equities and, by extension, China (FMT, Sept 4).
- **The Fed:** Governor **Christopher Waller** said he is leaning toward **keeping rates steady** at this month's meeting if inflation data keeps moderating — a supportive signal that helped HK shares rebound (FMT, Sept 4).
- **Tariffs (background, July):** The US announced **10–12.5% tariffs on goods from major trading partners, with Chinese exports facing 12.5%**, lifting China's effective US tariff rate to **22.2%** — well above the global average. That July move (and the **May Trump–Xi Beijing summit**, which focused on trade, tariffs, and export controls) frames the trade-tension overhang that continues to weigh on Chinese exporters and sentiment.

Beijing treated the selloff as a policy priority, prioritising stability over letting the correction run its course (TradingView/Reuters, July 20).

- **Margin/leverage:** Leveraged traders have been **unwinding margin debt** as rising global bond yields and a lingering oil shock added caution; at the same time, exchanges **raised the minimum margin requirement** to cool leverage after the market "overheated" on record turnover earlier in the rally. ## 6. Background: The July Tech Rout and the Valuation Reckoning

This is the context that makes the current market fragile. In **mid-July**, Chinese tech/chip stocks suffered a brutal two-week rout (Asia Times, July 23; TradingView, July 20):

- **Triggers:** (1) the debut of **Kimi K3**, a Chinese AI model (by Moonshot) offering competitive performance at far lower cost than Claude/ChatGPT — which shook global chip stocks and prompted the US to look into whether Chinese models were "distilled" from US ones; (2) the **CXMT $8.6B IPO** absorbing liquidity; (3) a **global chip selloff** (SanDisk -11%, Seagate -10%, TSMC -7.3%); and (4) the **Korea rate hike** triggering margin calls.
- **Damage:** ~**10 trillion yuan ($1.48T)** of market cap wiped out in two weeks; the **STAR Market fell ~25% from its July 1 peak**.
- **The valuation problem:** Chinese chip stocks were extremely overvalued — the **top-11 A-share semiconductor companies averaged a ~122x P/E** (Moore Threads ~2,560x, Hua Hong Grace ~1,039x) versus **Nvidia ~32.5x** and **TSMC ~35–37x**. As one analyst put it: "The era of buying any chip stock and expecting a surge is over."
- **The rescue:** State funds (national team) deployed 60B yuan+ and the CSRC held stability meetings — but the support "lasted only two to three days," and chip stocks kept sliding.

**Why it matters now:** The AI/tech de-rating that capped this week's A-share gains is the continuation of that July valuation reckoning. The market has not fully digested stretched tech valuations, which is why it remains sensitive to US yields and flow shifts.
