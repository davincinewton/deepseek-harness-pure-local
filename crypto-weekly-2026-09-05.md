## 1. Markets: Bitcoin's Third Straight Weekly Gain

Bitcoin closed the week up **4.6%**, reaching an overnight high of **$82,272.31** — its highest level since the **May 11** peak of $82,499.99 — before last trading near **$81,151** (CNBC, Sept 4). After trading in a **$60K–$70K** range for much of the year, BTC broke above $70K in late August and has climbed ever since.

The driver is the **"debasement trade"** — traders rotating out of dollars into crypto and gold. As Dominika Nestarcova, Goldman Sachs executive director of digital assets, put it:

> "The price breakout came towards the [end of August] with the re-emergence of the debasement trade re-igniting investor interest in BTC — the U.S. Treasury announced it would increase purchases of longer-dated Treasuries, long yields fell, the dollar weakened, and BTC and gold moved higher."

**Context (prior week, for reference):** The week ending **Aug 21** saw Bitcoin's biggest weekly gain in two years (to ~$79K), which The Block described as "genuinely broad-based" — a signal, per Nexo's Iliya Kalchev, because "narrow rallies tend to be the ones that don't last." This week extended that move.

## 3. Regulation: The CLARITY Act — Momentum vs. Delay Risk

This is the week's most consequential story, and the sources **genuinely disagree on timing**.

**The optimistic case (Crypto News, Sept 2):** SEC Chair Paul Atkins said the CLARITY Act could advance through the Senate **within two weeks**, ahead of a **cloture vote on Sept 15** (the motion ripens at 2:15 p.m. ET). Cloture needs **60 votes**; Republicans hold 53, so leaders must win over at least seven Democrats. The House passed its version **294–134 in July 2025**; the Senate Banking Committee advanced its version **15–9 in May 2026** (two Democrats crossing over). The bill would split SEC/CFTC authority — SEC over securities, CFTC over digital commodities and part of the spot market — and create registration and AML requirements.

**The skeptical case (TradingView, Sept 3):** House Republicans **canceled the final two weeks of the September session**; the House returns Sept 14 for only a four-day session before leaving until after the midterms. Even if the Senate acts Sept 15, the House must still pass the Senate version. Galaxy Digital's Alex Thorn called pre-midterm passage **"extremely unlikely,"** and one trader warned it is "potentially very bad news for the crypto industry." A post-midterm delay adds uncertainty given Democrats are likely to win the House.

**The market's read:** Kalshi traders price 2026 passage at **49%** — almost evenly split. (Polymarket's estimate swung from 82% in February to ~16% in early August before recovering.)

**The SEC's fallback:** Regardless of the bill, the SEC proposed **Regulation Crypto Assets on Aug 18** — a $5M startup exemption, a $75M fundraising exemption, and a safe harbor letting certain tokens exit securities status. Atkins stressed that rules need "statutory grounding" to be durable, and CFTC Chair Michael Selig said the CFTC will develop its own rules either way.

> ⚠️ **Conflict (flagged):** Atkins ("within weeks") vs. Thorn ("extremely unlikely before midterms") vs. Kalshi (49%). The disagreement is real and hinges on the Senate–House sequencing and the shortened House calendar.

## 4. Ethereum: Corporate Accumulation Drives the Narrative

ETH hit a high of **$2,545.62** on Friday (its best since Aug 27; CNBC) and rallied **55% during August** (CoinDesk). But the week's ETH story is **BitMine Immersion Technologies**:

- Bought **53,501 ETH (~$131.3M)** last week — a **65th consecutive week** of purchases since launching its treasury strategy in June 2025.
- Holdings now **5,901,112 ETH (~$14.5B)**, about **4.9% of Ethereum's 120.7M supply** — roughly **134,000 ETH short** of its self-imposed 5% target (~6.04M).
- Staked ~**5.07M ETH (86% of holdings)**, projecting ~**$335M** in annualized staking revenue.

Chairman **Tom Lee** framed the strong quarter as a setup for institutions:

> "We believe this sets the stage for institutions to add to their crypto holdings given the substantial outperformance of crypto versus other macro assets in 3Q so far."

Lee also cited a potential September market-structure bill vote, renewed Korean investor demand, tokenization, and AI-agent blockchain use as catalysts.
